User guide

How to upload files and publish them as Confluence pages with Miksumia Publisher.

What the app does

Drop up to five files, choose where they go in Confluence, and each one becomes a Confluence page. Word, PowerPoint, Excel, PDF and images are read by the app's own readers, without AI: headings, lists, tables, images and links come out as real Confluence content, so Confluence search and Rovo find every word.

The app adds nothing visible to your pages (no banner, no label), and it keeps nothing of your files.

It does not connect to Google Drive, does not synchronise anything, does not translate and does not use AI. You upload files by hand, and each upload is final: the app never updates a page afterwards.

Install and permissions

A site administrator installs the app from the Atlassian Marketplace. After that, anyone who can create a page in a space can publish there with the app. The app checks your Confluence permission on the place you choose, and Confluence checks it again when the page is created: you can never publish where you could not create a page by hand.

The first time you use the app, Confluence asks you to allow it to act on your behalf ("Allow access"): that is what lets the app check your permission. Confluence may ask again after an update of the app that needs new permissions. The app's "Help" link opens this guide.

Publish files, step by step

Step 1 — Files

Drag files onto the frame, or click "Or choose files". Each file shows an icon for its type. A file the app cannot take is refused at once, with the reason, before anything is sent:

CaseReason shown
a type the app does not readthis type of file is not supported
over 50 MBover the 50 MB limit
an empty fileis empty
a name over 255 charactersthe name is too long
a sixth file, and beyondan upload holds 5 at most

Scanned PDFs and workbooks can only be judged once read: they are checked after the upload, and the screen says so.

Step 2 — Where (from the Apps menu only)

Choose a space (each one shows the Confluence icon). Under it, the frame "Parent page or folder" is there from the start, saying "Choose a space to see its pages". Once a space is chosen, the frame shows its pages in the same order as in Confluence's sidebar, folders included, and they open level by level. The space's home page is the first row.

One field, "Filter by title", sits at the top of the frame. Empty, you see the tree. From two letters on, the pages and folders whose title starts with what you typed replace the tree's rows; pick one there, then clear the field to come back to the tree. A line under the field reminds you what the list shows.

A line reminds you where the pages will go, as "space › page". The app tells you at once if you cannot create a page at the place you chose. If it could not read your permission, it says so and offers "Check again".

Step 3 — Publish

A sentence reminds you how many files go under which place. Click "Publish". The screen then says what it is doing, file by file: "Uploading…", "Uploaded, waiting its turn", "Reading the file…", "Creating the page…", then "Published".

When it is done, a green block says how many pages were published and under which place, with an "Open the page" link on each. Files that could not be published are listed under it in an orange block, with their reason; if none could, a red block says "Nothing was published". "Upload more files" starts again.

When some files could not be sent or started, "Try again with these files" goes back to the last step with those files only, for the same place.

You can go back from step 2 to step 1, and from step 3 to either, until you click Publish. The screen's heading says where you are ("Step 2 of 3: Where").

What gets published

FileBecomes
Word (.docx)one page: headings, lists, tables, images, links, footnotes
PowerPoint (.pptx)one page, one Confluence tab per slide
Excel (.xlsx)one page, one Confluence tab per sheet
PDFone page, one Confluence tab per PDF page
PNG, JPEGan image page

A Google Doc, Sheet or Slides file can be published by downloading it first as .docx, .xlsx or .pptx.

  • The page title is the file name without its extension. If a page of the space has it already, a number is added: "Report (2)".
  • Scanned PDFs. A PDF of 3 pages or fewer is published even if it is entirely scanned (an attestation, a receipt, a signed page). Beyond 3 pages, a PDF whose pages are more than half scans is refused, and the screen says why. A scanned page is published as its picture, so its words are not searchable; a PDF that carries a text layer (OCR done beforehand) is read in full.
  • Workbooks. A workbook with no sheet small enough for a page is refused, and the screen says why: no empty page is ever created.
  • Parts the app cannot convert (a chart type, an embedded object) and missing pictures are left out, and a line under the file says so.
  • Words the source hides (hidden sheets, hidden text) are never published: the app publishes what the editor shows, not what the file stores.

Your files and your data

Your file never passes through the app's functions: your browser puts it in the app's temporary storage, inside Atlassian; a background task reads it, publishes the page, and erases the file. A file left behind expires by itself within one hour. The result (the page link and its warnings) is kept only until your screen has read it, 24 hours at most.

Nothing of your documents is kept: no copy, no extracted text, no log of their content. The pages created are yours, like any page of your space, and they stay if the app is uninstalled. Your documents never leave Atlassian. Details in our privacy policy.

Built on

The app is built on skryba, its open-source document reader (AGPL-3.0): npmjs.com/package/skryba.

Three of skryba's reading decisions come from Side Liu, Jiangpeng Liu, Jinwen Xin, Guojun Peng and Jiang Ming, "Beyond the Editing Canvas: Evidence Divergence in OOXML-to-LLM Ingestion", arXiv:2608.25880, starting with the one above: publish what the editor shows, never what the file stores.
